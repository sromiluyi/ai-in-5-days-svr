## Section 1: Short Answer Questions

### Question 1: What act does Katniss perform that begins her journey in the Hunger Games, and whom does she protect?
During the annual District 12 reaping for the 74th Hunger Games, twelve-year-old Primrose Everdeen's name is unexpectedly drawn from the glass bowl despite having only one slip entered. In an immediate act of selfless love and defiance against the ritual, sixteen-year-old Katniss Everdeen pushes past the Peacekeepers and screams, "I volunteer! I volunteer as tribute!" to take her sister's place.

### Question 2: How does Katniss use the tracker jacker nest defensively when surrounded by the Career tributes in the tree?
Trapped high in a tree with the Careers and Peeta waiting below, Katniss notices Rue quietly gesturing toward a golden tracker jacker nest above. Recognizing that the venomous muttations could eliminate her enemies, Katniss waits for the loud Capitol anthem to saw through the branch holding the nest. When it plummets into the Careers' camp, the lethal stings kill Glimmer and the District 4 girl, scattering the survivors and enabling Katniss to retrieve Glimmer's silver bow and arrows.

### Question 3: Explain the significance of the four-note Mockingjay song that Katniss and Rue use to communicate in the arena.
Rue teaches Katniss a four-note melody that she previously used in District 11 orchards to indicate the end of the shift. In the arena, the girls agree to whistle this tune to the native mockingjays so the birds carry the notes across the forest canopy, verifying that both allies remain unharmed. The whistle symbolizes inter-district solidarity and reclaims a Capitol surveillance creature (the jabberjay hybrid) as an instrument of human connection and resistance.

## Section 2: Long Essay Questions

### Question 4: How does the Capitol use the Hunger Games as an instrument of psychological, political, and physical control over the twelve districts? Use evidence from the novel.
The Capitol maintains total hegemony over Panem by employing the Hunger Games as a weapon of physical terror, political subjugation, and psychological fragmentation. Physically, the annual sacrifice of twenty-four innocent children serves as a visceral reminder of the Capitol's victory in the Dark Days and its absolute power over life and death. The Treaty of Treason codifies this forced servitude, proving that no family or child is safe from Capitol extraction.

Politically and psychologically, the Games weaponize entertainment to fracture solidarity among the districts. By transforming children into gladiators forced to slaughter one another, the Capitol fosters bitter inter-district hatred; districts view each other as competitors for survival rather than comrades suffering under shared oppression. Furthermore, mandatory Capitol broadcasts force starving families in District 12 to watch Capitol citizens treat their children's agony as spectacle, extinguishing hope and enforcing psychological paralysis.

### Question 5: In a well-developed essay, discuss how Katniss's honoring of Rue with flowers and the climax with the nightlock berries represent subversion against the Capitol.
Throughout *The Hunger Games*, Katniss discovers that preserving personal morality in the face of death is the ultimate form of rebellion against totalitarianism. Following Rue's murder by Marvel, Katniss refuses to abandon her fallen ally as disposable arena fodder. Instead, she sings to Rue as she dies and weaves a blanket of vibrant yellow and purple wildflowers around her body, covering the fatal wound. By performing this funeral rite on live television, Katniss strips the Capitol of its dehumanizing narrative, publicly declaring that Rue possessed inherent dignity and worth. This brave act elicits an unprecedented gift: a loaf of dark bread from District 11, marking the first time in Games history that one district provided aid to a tribute from another.

This defiant philosophy reaches its peak during the climax at the Cornucopia with the poisonous nightlock berries, directly fulfilling Peeta's pre-Games assertion that he wants to "die as myself" and prove the Capitol does not own him. When the Gamemakers abruptly revoke the rule permitting two victors, Peeta urges Katniss to kill him. Katniss refuses to become the Capitol's executioner. Recognizing that the Capitol cannot survive a Games without a victor, Katniss places the lethal berries in Peeta's hand and her own. By counting to three and preparing to swallow the poison simultaneously, Katniss and Peeta hold the Capitol hostage, exposing President Snow's vulnerability and forcing the Gamemakers to crown both as victors. Both the floral memorial and the berry gambit prove that human compassion and collective defiance can shatter Capitol control.
