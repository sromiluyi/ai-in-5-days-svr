## Section 1: Short Answer Questions

### Question 1: What act does Katniss perform that begins her journey in the Hunger Games, and whom does she protect?
At the District 12 reaping, Prim's name was drawn from the bowl. Katniss loves her sister more than anything, so she stepped forward and yelled that she volunteers as tribute to take Prim's place.

### Question 2: How does Katniss use the tracker jacker nest defensively when surrounded by the Career tributes in the tree?
When Katniss was stuck in the tree, Rue pointed out a tracker jacker nest above her head. Katniss decided to saw through the branch while the Capitol anthem was playing so they wouldn't hear her. The nest dropped on the Careers, stinging them and killing Glimmer. Katniss was able to grab Glimmer's bow and arrows.

### Question 3: Explain the significance of the four-note Mockingjay song that Katniss and Rue use to communicate in the arena.
Rue learned the four-note whistle back in District 11 to signal the end of work. In the arena, Katniss and Rue whistle to the mockingjays so the birds repeat the tune to let each other know they are still alive and safe. It shows they are friends working together even though the Capitol wants them to fight.

## Section 2: Long Essay Questions

### Question 4: How does the Capitol use the Hunger Games as an instrument of psychological, political, and physical control over the twelve districts? Use evidence from the novel.
The Capitol uses the Hunger Games to completely control the people in Panem. Physically, the Capitol proves its power by taking 24 children every single year as punishment for the rebellion during the Dark Days. The districts are powerless to stop them because Peacekeepers enforce the laws with violence.

Psychologically, the Games are designed to turn the districts against each other. Instead of realizing they could unite against President Snow, each district cheers for their own tributes and sees other districts as enemies. Forcing families to watch their children fight to the death on television makes everyone feel terrified and hopeless.

### Question 5: In a well-developed essay, discuss how Katniss's honoring of Rue with flowers and the climax with the nightlock berries represent subversion against the Capitol.
When Rue dies after being speared by Marvel, Katniss does something that changes the Games. Instead of walking away, she covers Rue's body in wildflowers and sings to her. This shows the Capitol that Rue was a human being and not just a piece in their deadly game. This action moves District 11 so much that they send Katniss a loaf of bread, showing that people from different districts can care about each other.

Later at the Cornucopia, Katniss and Peeta use the poisonous nightlock berries to outsmart the Gamemakers. Peeta said before the Games that he wanted to die as himself and show the Capitol didn't own him. By getting ready to eat the berries together, they refuse to kill each other. The Capitol is forced to let them both live because they cannot afford to have no victor at all. Both of these moments prove that compassion and bravery can defeat Capitol tyranny.
